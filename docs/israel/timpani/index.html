About Hatikvah

Naftali Herz Imber's poem "Tikvatenu" (Our Hope) was set in 1888 by Samuel Cohen to a melody he knew from the Romanian folk song "Carul cu boi", a descendant of the Renaissance tune "La Mantovana" that Smetana also used in "Vltava". The official text is the first stanza and the refrain: "Kol od balevav penimah" (As long as in the heart, within). It is one of the few national anthems in a minor key.

About this orchestral arrangement

Hope as a slow burn in E minor: a restrained, klezmer-tinged solo clarinet over harp and a cello pedal, a tender stanza for the cellos and violins, then a river of string sixteenths in the spirit of Smetana's "Moldau" under the tune, growing to a grand final statement of choir, horns and trumpets that ends on an E major chord.

The melody is the official tune, note for note; only the orchestration is new. E minor, 4/4, 2:31, 21 parts.

Who wrote Hatikvah?

The music is by Samuel Cohen (1888), after the Moldavian/Romanian folk song "Carul cu boi" and the words by Naftali Herz Imber (1878). It was adopted Anthem by custom from 1948; by law (amendment to the Flag and Emblem Law) on 10 November 2004.

What inspired the orchestration?

Hope as a slow burn in E minor: a restrained, klezmer-tinged solo clarinet over harp and a cello pedal, a tender stanza for the cellos and violins, then a river of string sixteenths in the spirit of Smetana's "Moldau" under the tune, growing to a grand final statement of choir, horns and trumpets that ends on an E major chord.

What key and tempo is it in?

This arrangement is in E minor, 4/4 and starts at about 66 beats per minute; it lasts 2:31.
