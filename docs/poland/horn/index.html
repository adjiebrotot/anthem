About Mazurek Dąbrowskiego

Józef Wybicki wrote the song in 1797 in Reggio Emilia, Italy, for the Polish Legions of General Jan Henryk Dąbrowski, at a time when Poland had been partitioned off the map. "Jeszcze Polska nie zginęła, kiedy my żyjemy": Poland has not yet perished, so long as we still live. It became the anthem in 1926-1927.

About this orchestral arrangement

A mazurka cavalry charge: a 3/4 mazurka with the stress on the second beat, galloping strings, and the refrain "Marsz, marsz, Dąbrowski" as a brass charge.

The melody is the official tune, note for note; only the orchestration is new. F major, 3/4, 0:47, 22 parts.

Who wrote Mazurek Dąbrowskiego?

The music is by a Polish folk mazurka and the words by Józef Wybicki (1797). It was adopted 1926-1927.

What inspired the orchestration?

A mazurka cavalry charge: a 3/4 mazurka with the stress on the second beat, galloping strings, and the refrain "Marsz, marsz, Dąbrowski" as a brass charge.
