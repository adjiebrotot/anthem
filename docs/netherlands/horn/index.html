About Het Wilhelmus

"Het Wilhelmus" is often called the oldest national anthem in the world: its words date from 1568-1572, the start of the Dutch Revolt, and its tune from a French song of 1568. It is a song in the voice of William of Orange, "Wilhelmus van Nassouwe", the father of the nation; it became the official anthem in 1932. Usually the first and sixth stanzas are sung.

About this orchestral arrangement

A Golden Age consort: oboes, bassoons and a lute-like harp in the colours of the 1600s, then an organ-like brass chorale with choir.

The melody is the official tune, note for note; only the orchestration is new. G major, 4/4, 1:45, 21 parts.

Who wrote Het Wilhelmus?

The music is by a French melody of 1568 and the words by unknown (often attributed to Philips of Marnix), 1568-1572. It was adopted 1932.
