About Kimigayo

"Kimigayo" has one of the oldest anthem texts in the world: a short waka poem from the 10th-century Kokin Wakashū, wishing that the reign may last a thousand, eight thousand generations, until pebbles grow into boulders covered with moss. The court musician Hayashi Hiromori set it in 1880, with harmony by the German bandmaster Franz Eckert; it became the official anthem by law in 1999.

About this orchestral arrangement

From piano to temple: a solo piano plays the tune first, quietly, then the orchestra enters in a broad unison tutti with choir, horns and trumpets.

The melody is the official tune, note for note; only the orchestration is new. D modal (C signature), 4/4, 1:42, 18 parts.

1 part: Clarinets. Parts are written for the clarinet in B♭, so they read a transposition above the sounding pitch; the full score is in concert pitch.

Is the clarinet sheet music of Kimigayo free to download?

Can I listen to it while I read?

Yes. Press play above the score: the full orchestra plays in your browser and the notes light up as they sound, with the page scrolling along. Click any note to play from there, or slow it down to practise.

Who wrote Kimigayo?

The music is by Hayashi Hiromori (1880), harmonised by Franz Eckert and the words by a waka poem from the Kokin Wakashū (10th century). It was adopted by law in 1999 (in use since 1880).

Is the melody the official one?

Yes. The tune is the official melody, note for note, checked against published scores; only the orchestration is new.

What key and tempo is it in?

This arrangement is in D modal (C signature), 4/4 and starts at about 56 beats per minute; it lasts 1:42.
