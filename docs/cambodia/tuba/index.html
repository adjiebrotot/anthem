About Nokoreach

"Nokoreach" (Majestic Kingdom) sets words by the Venerable Chuon Nath, Cambodia's Supreme Patriarch, to a melody drawn from a traditional Khmer tune. It was adopted in 1941, set aside in the 1970s, and restored with the monarchy in 1993. The words ask heaven to protect the king and recall the temples of Angkor and the glory of the Khmer people.

About this orchestral arrangement

Inspired by Angkor: a slow processional of deep bass drum and cymbal strokes, like a temple gong, and a harp ostinato, with the pentatonic tune growing from the flutes to an oboe and clarinet duet, then to choir and brass.

The melody is the official tune, note for note; only the orchestration is new. Ab major, 4/4, 1:28, 18 parts.

Who wrote Nokoreach?

The music is by based on a Khmer folk melody, arranged by F. Perruchot and J. Jekyll (1941) and the words by Chuon Nath. It was adopted 1941, re-adopted 1993.
