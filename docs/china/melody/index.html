About March of the Volunteers

Written for the 1935 film "Children of Troubled Times" during the war against Japan, the "March of the Volunteers" calls on those who refuse to be slaves to rise up and build a new Great Wall with their flesh and blood. It was chosen as the provisional anthem in 1949 and written into the Constitution in 2004.

About this orchestral arrangement

A rising tide: a galloping low-string ostinato drives the march, trumpet calls and heavy trombones carry the tune, and the choir is held back until the final "前进！" (March on!).

The melody is the official tune, note for note; only the orchestration is new. G major, 2/4, 0:45, 13 parts.

Who wrote March of the Volunteers?

The music is by Nie Er (1935) and the words by Tian Han (1934). It was adopted 1949 (provisional), in the Constitution since 2004.

What inspired the orchestration?

A rising tide: a galloping low-string ostinato drives the march, trumpet calls and heavy trombones carry the tune, and the choir is held back until the final "前进！" (March on!).

What key and tempo is it in?

This arrangement is in G major, 2/4 and starts at about 88 beats per minute; it lasts 0:45.
