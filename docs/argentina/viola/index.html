About Himno Nacional Argentino

Written during the struggle for independence from Spain, the "Marcha Patriótica" was approved by the Assembly of the Year XIII on 11 May 1813, still celebrated as Anthem Day. It opens with "Oíd, mortales" (Hear, mortals) and the cry of freedom, and its chorus swears to live crowned with glory or to die with it. Today only the first and last lines of the poem and the chorus are sung, in Juan Pedro Esnaola's official 1928 version.

About this orchestral arrangement

Inspired by the Italian opera overtures of Rossini and Verdi, the style the anthem was born in. Clarinets and oboes sing the verse over a plucked, guitar-like string accompaniment, a Rossini crescendo builds the tension, and the chorus becomes a fast operatic finale with choir, trumpets and band percussion that broadens on the last "o juremos con gloria morir".

The melody is the official tune, note for note; only the orchestration is new. Bb major, 4/4, 2:44, 21 parts.

Who wrote Himno Nacional Argentino?

The music is by Blas Parera (1813) and the words by Vicente López y Planes (1812-1813). It was adopted 11 May 1813 (the shortened sung form by decree in 1900).

Is the melody the official one?

Yes. The tune is the official melody, note for note, checked against published scores; only the orchestration is new.
