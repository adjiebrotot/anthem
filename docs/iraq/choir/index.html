About Mawtini

The Palestinian poet Ibrahim Tuqan wrote "Mawtini" in 1934 and the Lebanese composer Muhammad Fuliefil set it to music the same year; the song spread across the Arab world as a patriotic hymn and served as Palestine's anthem until 1996. Iraq adopted it as its national anthem in 2004. This version plays all three stanzas, each opening and closing with "Mawtini, mawtini".

About this orchestral arrangement

The land of two rivers: a solo oboe taqsim over a drone, the harp as a qanun and frame drums in maqsum; oboe and clarinet in heterophony with the strings answering in unison, refrains opening into strings in three octaves over brass pedals, and a last refrain with choir and trumpets.

The melody is the official tune, note for note; only the orchestration is new. A minor, 4/4, 2:49, 21 parts.

Who wrote Mawtini?

The music is by Muhammad Fuliefil (Mohammed Flayfel, 1934) and the words by Ibrahim Tuqan (1934). It was adopted 2004 (earlier the de facto anthem of Palestine, from 1936).

What inspired the orchestration?

The land of two rivers: a solo oboe taqsim over a drone, the harp as a qanun and frame drums in maqsum; oboe and clarinet in heterophony with the strings answering in unison, refrains opening into strings in three octaves over brass pedals, and a last refrain with choir and trumpets.

What key and tempo is it in?

This arrangement is in A minor, 4/4 and starts at about 76 beats per minute; it lasts 2:49.
