About Kaba Ma Kyei

Saya Tin wrote "Kaba Ma Kyei" in 1930 as the song of the Dobama Asiayone ("We Burmans") movement and was jailed for it; the song became a symbol of the independence struggle and was adopted as the national anthem in 1947. The full version, sung at home, opens with a section in traditional Burmese style before the march-like main part; this version plays it complete, with the main part's first two lines sung twice.

About this orchestral arrangement

Golden pagodas at dawn: harp sweeps in the manner of the saung gauk (Burmese arched harp), a solo oboe as the hne and a flute as the palwei over a soft drone, the si-wa timekeepers and a pat waing-like timpani figure; then the march grows into a warm tutti of strings and horns and a broad choir finale, without snare.

The melody is the official tune, note for note; only the orchestration is new. Ab major, 2/4, 1:49, 21 parts.

Who wrote Kaba Ma Kyei?

The music is by Saya Tin (1930) and the words by Saya Tin (1930). It was adopted 22 September 1947 (official on independence, 1948); confirmed by the State Anthem Law of 2010.

What inspired the orchestration?

Golden pagodas at dawn: harp sweeps in the manner of the saung gauk (Burmese arched harp), a solo oboe as the hne and a flute as the palwei over a soft drone, the si-wa timekeepers and a pat waing-like timpani figure; then the march grows into a warm tutti of strings and horns and a broad choir finale, without snare.

What key and tempo is it in?

This arrangement is in Ab major, 2/4 and starts at about 66 beats per minute; it lasts 1:49.
