About Aash al-Malik

The melody was composed in 1947 and played without words for decades; Ibrahim Khafaji's text was adopted in 1984. "Sāri'ī lil-majdi wal-'alyā'", hasten to glory and supremacy, it begins, glorifying the Creator of the heavens and raising the green flag.

About this orchestral arrangement

From desert dawn to royal court: an open-fifth drone with a lone flute and horn call, a pulse of harp and plucked strings in the Arabic maqsum rhythm with bass drum and triangle, then a trumpet fanfare and a full second pass with choir.

The melody is the official tune, note for note; only the orchestration is new. F major, 4/4, 1:59, 19 parts.

Who wrote Aash al-Malik?

The music is by Abdul Rahman Al-Khateeb (1947) and the words by Ibrahim Khafaji (1984). It was adopted music 1950, words 1984.

What inspired the orchestration?

From desert dawn to royal court: an open-fifth drone with a lone flute and horn call, a pulse of harp and plucked strings in the Arabic maqsum rhythm with bass drum and triangle, then a trumpet fanfare and a full second pass with choir.

How does Aash al-Malik compare with the other anthems here?

Its tune dates from 1947, the 8th newest of the 53 anthems on this site. At 1:59 this arrangement is the 17th longest, and with 19 parts it is among the largest scores here.

What key and tempo is it in?

This arrangement is in F major, 4/4 and starts at about 70 beats per minute; it lasts 1:59.
