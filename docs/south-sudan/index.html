About South Sudan Oyee!

South Sudan chose its anthem by a national competition in 2010, ahead of the independence referendum of January 2011; the winning music came from students and teachers of the University of Juba. "Oyee" is a cheer, "hurrah". The three stanzas pray for the land, raise the flag with the guiding star and honour the martyrs, and the anthem closes with "Oh God, bless South Sudan!"

About this orchestral arrangement

A new nation rising: an African drum ensemble in an interlocking 12/8 groove (bell, slit drums, frame drum, congas, shaker), a marimba-like piano ostinato and pizzicato strings, and call and response: the choir sings every line and the horns, trumpets and trombones answer, up to the full choir in harmony.

The melody is the official tune, note for note; only the orchestration is new. F major, 4/4, 1:34, 25 parts.

Who wrote South Sudan Oyee!?

The music is by students and faculty of the University of Juba (2011) and the words by written collectively (2011). It was adopted 9 July 2011, at independence.

What inspired the orchestration?

A new nation rising: an African drum ensemble in an interlocking 12/8 groove (bell, slit drums, frame drum, congas, shaker), a marimba-like piano ostinato and pizzicato strings, and call and response: the choir sings every line and the horns, trumpets and trombones answer, up to the full choir in harmony.
