About Nkosi Sikelel' iAfrika / Die Stem van Suid-Afrika

South Africa's anthem joins two songs: the hymn "Nkosi Sikelel' iAfrika" (God Bless Africa), composed by Enoch Sontonga in 1897, and "Die Stem van Suid-Afrika" (The Call of South Africa). Combined in 1997 after the end of apartheid, it is sung in five of the country's languages and ends "Let us live and strive for freedom in South Africa our land".

About this orchestral arrangement

Choir-led: a hummed hymn over a cello drone, horns in call and response, a brass and snare march for Die Stem, and a full tutti close in English.

The melody is the official tune, note for note; only the orchestration is new. G major to D major, 2/4, 2:09, 16 parts.

The music is by Enoch Sontonga (1897); Marthinus Lourens de Villiers (1921) and the words by Enoch Sontonga, Samuel Mqhayi; C. J. Langenhoven (1918); English text 1997. It was adopted 1997 (combined form).
