About La Marseillaise

Written in one night in Strasbourg in April 1792 as a war song for the Army of the Rhine, it took its name from the volunteers of Marseille who sang it marching into Paris. Rouget de Lisle's call "Aux armes, citoyens!" became the anthem of the Republic in 1795 and again, for good, in 1879.

About this orchestral arrangement

A revolutionary call to arms: a brisk march with a snare drum and a high flute, driving bouncing strings, trumpet calls and the choir for the refrain "Aux armes, citoyens".

The melody is the official tune, note for note; only the orchestration is new. G major, 4/4, 1:08, 16 parts.

Who wrote La Marseillaise?

The music is by Claude Joseph Rouget de Lisle (1792) and the words by Claude Joseph Rouget de Lisle (1792). It was adopted 14 July 1795, again in 1879.
